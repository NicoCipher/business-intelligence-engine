# Validation Intelligence

Version: 1.0

Status: Architectural Roadmap

---

# 1. Purpose

This document defines Validation Intelligence.

Validation Intelligence is the subsystem responsible for determining whether previously generated intelligence was correct.

Unlike the primary intelligence engine, which generates assessments, Validation Intelligence evaluates those assessments using future evidence.

Its purpose is continuous learning.

---

# 2. Philosophy

Every recommendation is a hypothesis.

Time eventually provides additional evidence.

Validation Intelligence compares:

Predicted Reality

↓

Observed Reality

↓

Learning

BIA should become more accurate because history exists.

---

# 3. Objectives

Validation Intelligence exists to achieve five goals.

1. Measure prediction quality.

2. Improve future recommendations.

3. Increase confidence calibration.

4. Detect systematic reasoning errors.

5. Build institutional learning.

---

# 4. Validation Cycle

Validation follows a continuous loop.

```
Recommendation

↓

Observation

↓

Evidence

↓

Validation

↓

Learning

↓

Improved Recommendation
```

This cycle never ends.

---

# 5. Validation Targets

Future validation may evaluate:

- market demand
- customer adoption
- funding activity
- hiring activity
- product launches
- competitive movement
- pricing changes
- regulatory changes

Validation measures reality rather than opinion.

---

# 6. Validation Outcomes

Every recommendation eventually reaches one of four states.

Confirmed

Evidence strongly supports the recommendation.

---

Partially Confirmed

Some evidence supports the recommendation.

Further observation is required.

---

Inconclusive

Insufficient evidence exists.

No conclusion should be drawn.

---

Rejected

Evidence contradicts the recommendation.

Historical reasoning remains preserved.

---

# 7. Learning

Validation exists to improve future intelligence.

Learning may influence:

- confidence
- evidence weighting
- canonicalization
- future recommendations

Learning never modifies historical observations.

---

# 8. Confidence Calibration

Confidence should become calibrated through validation.

Repeated successful recommendations justify stronger future confidence.

Repeated failures justify more conservative confidence.

Calibration occurs through evidence rather than manual tuning.

---

# 9. Historical Preservation

Failed recommendations are valuable.

Rejected intelligence provides information about incorrect assumptions.

Historical failures remain part of institutional memory.

BIA learns from incorrect conclusions.

---

# 10. Future Workspace

Validation Intelligence will eventually expose dedicated workflows.

Examples include:

- active hypotheses
- pending validation
- validated opportunities
- rejected opportunities
- evidence timeline

These workflows extend the intelligence platform.

---

# 11. Separation of Responsibilities

The Intelligence Engine generates recommendations.

Validation Intelligence evaluates recommendations.

Neither subsystem replaces the other.

---

# 12. Future Extensions

Future capabilities may include:

- automatic validation detection
- validation scoring
- hypothesis aging
- prediction benchmarking
- confidence recalibration
- evidence quality weighting

These extend the validation architecture.

---

# 13. Authority

This document defines the architectural principles governing Validation Intelligence.

Future validation engines, prediction systems, confidence calibration mechanisms, and autonomous learning agents must preserve these principles.
